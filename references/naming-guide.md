# Naming guide

Use the current naming brief to generate varied candidates. Do not reuse a fixed list of names or assume a particular industry, audience, or metaphor.

## Generation techniques

Use several techniques per batch, guided by the user's direction:

1. **Semantic exploration.** Identify concrete nouns, verbs, and concepts connected to the product's purpose and desired qualities.
2. **Compounds.** Combine two short, meaningful word parts. Keep the result easy to pronounce and spell.
3. **Coined words.** Blend or shorten relevant words while preserving a clear pronunciation. Avoid constructions that need a spelling explanation.
4. **Audience language.** Explore expressions familiar to the intended customers, matching the product's tone and seriousness.
5. **Metaphors.** Draw from a field or trait that fits the brief. Explain the connection rather than choosing a metaphor solely because it sounds appealing.

Use the user's exclusions and constraints. Do not impose an industry-specific vocabulary or naming style when none was requested.

## Quality bar

- **Length:** Prefer 8 letters or fewer, with 10 as a default maximum; explicit user preferences take precedence.
- **Radio test:** A listener should be able to spell the name after hearing it once. Avoid ambiguous spellings.
- **Characters:** Avoid hyphens and digits unless requested.
- **Meaning:** Consider unintended meanings in the audience's languages. Search when uncertain; do not claim exhaustive language checks.
- **Collisions:** Search finalists alongside the relevant product category and audience. Flag confusingly similar brands using current evidence. Explain relevant conflicts; an unrelated use in another industry is not automatically disqualifying. This is not trademark clearance.
- **Fit:** Explain each finalist in one line using the brief's desired qualities.
- **Variety:** Keep multiple naming approaches in a batch instead of filling it with close synonyms.

## Availability strategy

Short common words face more competition. Include less common words, compounds, and pronounceable coined names, according to the brief. These are generation heuristics; only live checks establish registration status.

Check every requested TLD and rank viable candidates using the user's preference order, naming fit, budget, and confirmed pricing. Do not assume a name is available because of its shape or length.

## Presenting a pick

Give the domain, why it fits, registration evidence, price evidence, and a registrar link. Distinguish annual list estimates from confirmed upfront charges and minimum registration terms. Explain unusual meanings when relevant. Mention alternate TLDs when they offer a useful fallback.
